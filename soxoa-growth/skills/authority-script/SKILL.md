# Authority script

A speaker sounds like an authority when they say something specific that they know first-hand and then stand behind it. They sound lost when the script opens with a question, reports what "people are saying" and hedges every line. This skill rewrites a short script so the speaker leads with what they actually know.

## Inputs

- The script, and where it will be used: platform, length, to camera or voiceover.
- What the speaker has done on this subject: what they built, tested, measured or saw, with numbers and dates where they exist. Ask for this. It is the raw material.
- A sample of how they talk, if available, so the rewrite sounds like them.

## Method

1. **Find the weak signals in the draft.** Mark each one:
   - opening with a question or with "so" and a wind-up
   - second-hand framing: "apparently", "I read that", "people are saying"
   - hedges stacked on a claim: "kind of", "I think maybe", "it seems like"
   - news recited with no view on it
   - explaining a term the audience knows
   - ending on "what do you think?"
2. **Find the claim.** One sentence the speaker believes and can support from their own work. Not a summary of the news: a position on it. If there is none, ask what they would tell a friend who asked "so what should I do about this?"
3. **Find the proof.** The first-hand detail that earns the claim: "I ran this on five of my own sites last week", a number they measured, a mistake they made. One strong specific beats three general ones.
4. **Rebuild in this order.**
   - Line one: the claim or the most surprising specific. No greeting.
   - Next: why they are the one to say it, in a clause, through what they did. Not a title.
   - Middle: two or three points. Each is a plain statement followed by what it means for the viewer.
   - Close: one concrete action the viewer can take today, or the one thing to stop doing.
5. **Write for the mouth.** Short sentences. One idea each. Words the speaker uses. Read it aloud and cut anything that trips. Mark the beats where they should pause.
6. **Keep honest uncertainty, once.** Where something is not known, say so in one clear line: "Nobody outside the company knows how this is ranked yet. Here is what I can see." That reads as command of the subject. Hedging every sentence does not.
7. **Check every factual line** against what the speaker supplied. Anything unverified is flagged for them to confirm or cut.
8. **Give the timing.** Word count and estimated length at their speaking pace, with a cut-down version if it runs over.

## Rules

- Authority comes from true specifics. No invented credentials, client names, results or numbers.
- Do not turn uncertainty into false confidence. State what is known, what is not, and what the speaker would do anyway.
- Keep their voice. If they would never say a line, it does not go in.
- No attacks on named people or companies to sound strong.

## Output

The rewritten script with pause marks, a second version with a different opening line, the list of weak signals removed, the facts the speaker must confirm before recording, and the word count and estimated length.
